# Source shapes, red flags and citation styles

## Shapes by source type

| Type | Sections | Good signs | Red flags |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research paper | problem and why it matters; method (data, approach); results (key numbers, patterns); analysis (meaning, comparison with prior work); discussion (implications, limits, next work) | peer review, a method a reader could repeat, uncertainty reported (intervals, p-values), limits stated, conflicts disclosed | no method section, claims without data, funder that gains from one result, a venue known for paid publication without review |
| Article, post, news | claim; evidence offered; implication for the reader | named author with relevant background, linked sources, other views acknowledged, clear date | no author, no sources, headline stronger than the body, undisclosed sponsorship or affiliate links |
| Report, whitepaper | bottom line in two or three sentences; scope; key data; how data was gathered; recommendations; relevance to the reader | method disclosed, sample size and make-up stated, a known research body | a product pitch dressed as research, one small unrepresentative sample, conclusions beyond the data |
| Documentation | purpose; main parts or functions; requirements; limits and known issues; version and date | versioned, dated, examples that match the text | undated, version unknown, examples that contradict the text |

Several sources on one theme can also be written thematically: themes as headings, each source's contribution under each, then gaps.

## Citation styles

Pick by field: APA 7 (default; social science, business, product), IEEE (engineering, computing), Chicago notes and bibliography (humanities, history), Harvard (general academic, common in the UK and Australia), MLA 9 (arts, humanities).

Key differences, described rather than templated:
- APA 7: surname and initials, year in brackets after the authors, sentence-case title, journal and volume in italics, DOI as a full link. In text: surname and year; "et al." from three authors.
- IEEE: numbered in order of first citation, square-bracket numbers in text, initials before surname, title in quotes, abbreviated journal name, volume, number, pages, month and year, DOI last.
- Chicago: footnotes with full first name first; a bibliography sorted by surname with full stops between parts.
- Harvard: author and year order like APA; article titles in single quotes; in text, surname and year without a comma before the year.
- MLA 9: surname first, full first name, title in quotes, container in italics, "vol." and "no.", year, "pp."; in text, surname and page with no comma.

If the user's institution has its own style guide, follow it over these notes.
