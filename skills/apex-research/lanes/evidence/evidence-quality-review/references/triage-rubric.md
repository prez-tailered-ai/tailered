# Rubric questions

Ask only the questions for dimensions in scope. Each answer that drives a score cites a locator.

1. Problem and question: How specific is the problem? Is the contribution worth having? Are scope and assumptions written down? Does the stated question fit the contribution claimed?
2. Literature and context: Is the key prior work covered? Is it synthesised, or only listed? Are the claimed gaps genuine? Do foundational and recent works both appear?
3. Method: Does the design answer the question? Is each choice justified? Could someone repeat the work from the description of materials, data, participants or variables? Are ethical and practical constraints admitted?
4. Data and evidence: Are the sources credible and fit for purpose? Is the sample or corpus big enough? Are inclusion, exclusion and preprocessing written down? Are missing data and bias discussed?
5. Analysis: Do the methods suit the data? Are baselines and controls fair? Where needed, are uncertainty, sensitivity or robustness checked? Are rival explanations considered?
6. Results and interpretation: Are results presented clearly? Do the claims stay within what was shown? Can a reader follow the tables, figures and metrics? Are null or negative results reported honestly?
7. Limitations and validity threats: Are limits concrete rather than boilerplate? Are internal, external, construct and conclusion validity covered? Is speculation kept apart from demonstrated results?
8. Writing and structure: Does the argument read easily? Is each section organised around the question? Are terms and notation defined?
9. Citations: Does each cited work back the claim it is attached to? Are primary sources preferred? Are reviews and preprints labelled? Are metadata and links right?
