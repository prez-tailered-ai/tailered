# Teardown templates

## Collection list (per competitor)

| Source type | Capture |
|---|---|
| Pricing page | model (per seat, usage, flat, free tier), entry / mid / top price, trial length, what each tier gates |
| Product and help docs | features per tier, platforms (web, mobile, desktop, API), integrations, security and compliance claims |
| Changelog or release notes | release cadence over the last year, themes |
| Case studies and logos | who they sell to (segment, size, industry) |
| Reviews (review sites, app stores) | a sample sorted by recency; tag each as praise, request, bug or UX complaint; count themes |
| Job postings | counts by function (engineering, sales and success, data, compliance); stack mentions |
| Search visibility | which pages rank (product, blog, docs), blog cadence and topics |
| Public discussion | recurring praise, complaints and requests |
| Status page, filings | uptime history; revenue and customer counts if public |

## Twelve dimensions (1 to 5)

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Features | core only, clear gaps | solid coverage | broad and distinctive |
| Pricing | opaque or costly | clear, market rate | open, flexible, fair |
| UX | high friction | works | low friction, well guided |
| Performance | slow or unreliable | acceptable | fast, high uptime |
| Docs | thin or stale | decent | complete and searchable |
| Support | slow, one channel | chat and email | round the clock, fast |
| Integrations | five or fewer | six to twenty-five | more than twenty-five or a deep ecosystem |
| Security | not mentioned | an audit claimed | audited reports and certifications published |
| Scale | no large-customer tier | mid-market ready | large-enterprise grade |
| Brand | generic | decent | sharp and distinct |
| Community | none | a forum or chat space | active, self-sustaining |
| Innovation | no recent releases | about quarterly | frequent and meaningful |

Evidence note per score, for example: "UX 2: 38 review mentions of confusing navigation (p12, p14); seven steps before first value per help docs (p20)."

Optional weights, only on request: default emphasis on features and UX; for large buyers raise security and support; for developer tools raise integrations and features; for small buyers raise pricing and UX; for regulated buyers raise security. Show raw and weighted totals side by side.

## Outputs

**Feature matrix.** Rows: core features, tiers, platforms. Columns: your product plus competitors. Cells 1 to 5; total out of 60 for the twelve dimensions.

**Pricing comparison.** Per competitor: model, price points, trial. Then: price leader, value leader, premium player, where you sit, two or three pricing openings.

**Four-quadrant analysis**, per competitor, three to five bullets each: their strengths, their weaknesses, openings for you, threats to you. Each bullet names its signal.

**Positioning map.** Two stated axes (for example simple to complex, low to high value), a position per product with the reason, empty and crowded areas.

**UX audit.** Onboarding (time to first value, steps to activation, card required, guidance), key workflows (steps, friction, your score against theirs), mobile (ratings, parity, top complaint and praise), navigation (search, shortcuts, in-app help).

**Actions.** Quick wins (up to a month, low effort), medium term (one to three months), strategic (three to twelve months). Each names the finding it rests on.

**Stakeholder outline**, seven parts: summary with threat level, top strength, top opening and recommended action; positioning map; scorecard; pricing; UX, three places they do better and three where you win; voice of the customer, top three complaints; action plan with raw data as an appendix.
