# Work-sample hire

Page pushed hiring for raw ability over credentials, and Google decided hires by committee rather than one manager. The guard: Google's own data, as Laszlo Bock reported (New York Times, 2013), found that brainteasers predicted nothing and grades mattered little a few years out. So the test is a short, paid work sample from real company work, scored on a rubric written before anyone is seen. Candidate data stays private and member data never enters the sample.

## When to Activate

- the company chooses between candidates for a role, contract or modeling job.
- A choice rests on a resume, school, title or brainteaser.
- The owner asks how to test someone, or says hire for ability or work sample.

Do not use for:
- writing the role's requirements: tailered-ai:ability-over-credential;
- writing the pitch or outreach note: tailered-ai:mission-pitch;
- testing one candidate across several kinds of work: tailered-ai:generalist-stretch-test;
- an outsider's access to code, data or keys: tailered-ai:bring-in-the-builders;
- finding the money to pay them: tailered-ai:find-it-inside.

## Inputs and outputs

Where to work: create a task folder, `artifacts/<skill name>-<YYYYMMDD>/` in the current project (this session's scratchpad if the project is read-only), and write the output there. Tables go in your reply.

Inputs: the role, the candidates, a real problem it would face and the reviewers.

Missing inputs never leave a blank. Put in a concrete proposal from what you know, mark it `proposal`, finish every step, and end with the one or two inputs that would change the result. A proposal is a real value: a dollar figure, a name, a date, a game, a list of likely items. Writing missing, pending, unknown, TBD or $X in its place is still a blank.

Output: `hire.md`: the work sample, the rubric, each reviewer's scores and the verdict per candidate.

## Steps

Lead the reply with the score table by candidate and reviewer, then the verdict line, detail after.

### 1. Write the work sample

Take a real company problem on a copied, scrubbed dataset with no member data, such as: price this NHL total from captured odds and explain last week's miss. Set a time cap (proposal: 3 hours) and pay (proposal: $150 flat, the owner approves).

Completion: the task, the scrubbed data, the time cap and the pay.

### 2. Write the rubric first

Four or five criteria, each scored 1 to 4, written and saved before any entry is read. School, titles, past employers and follower counts are not criteria.

Completion: a saved rubric dated before the first entry.

### 3. Score blind, twice

Two reviewers score each entry alone with names hidden; proposal: the owner and one session. A gap of 2 or more points is talked through in writing. Entries and scores stay in the task folder, not shared or reused.

Completion: two score sets per candidate, and each large gap resolved in writing.

### 4. Verdict

Write hire, change (a second sample on the weakest criterion) or stop per candidate, with the scores that decided it. The owner makes any offer; nothing here sends a message or an offer. Access to code, data or keys goes to tailered-ai:bring-in-the-builders.

Completion: a verdict word per candidate with its scores, and the owner named for the offer.

## Anti-Patterns

- Brainteasers or trivia questions.
- Choosing by resume, school or followers.
- Writing the rubric after reading the entries.
- Real member data in the sample.
