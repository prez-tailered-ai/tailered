# Ability over credential

Set the bar as abilities before anyone applies. Early Google screened hard for raw ability, with transcripts and brainteasers (reported). The guard came from Google itself: its people chief, Laszlo Bock, said in 2013 that brainteasers were a waste and grades predicted little past the first years. A credential stands in for an ability; name the ability and how it would show.

## When to Activate

- A role, contract or creator brief's requirements are written or reviewed, before anyone is screened.
- A brief asks for degrees, schools, employers, years or follower counts.
- The owner asks "what should the bar be".

Do not use for:
- choosing between candidates with a work sample: tailered-ai:work-sample-hire;
- testing one candidate across several kinds of work: tailered-ai:generalist-stretch-test;
- selling the role: tailered-ai:mission-pitch;
- an outsider's access, keys and terms: tailered-ai:bring-in-the-builders;
- a claimed betting record: tailered-ai:signal-or-noise.

Testing the candidates belongs to the Page set's work-sample-hire and the Gates set's work-sample-interview, built at the same time.

## Inputs and outputs

Where to work: create a task folder, `artifacts/<skill name>-<YYYYMMDD>/` in the current project (this session's scratchpad if the project is read-only), and write the output there. Tables go in your reply.

Inputs: the brief, its requirements, and the first month's work.

Missing inputs never leave a blank. Put in a concrete proposal from what you know, mark it `proposal`, finish every step, and end with the one or two inputs that would change the result. A proposal is a real value: a dollar figure, a name, a date, a game, a list of likely items. Writing missing, pending, unknown, TBD or $X in its place is still a blank.

Output: `bar.md`: old requirements, abilities, evidence, levels and the rewrite.

## Steps

Lead the reply with the requirements table, then the verdict line, then the detail.

### 1. Name the first month's work

Two or three real tasks the person would do in month one, such as fixing a new league team-name aliases or adding a rest-days input to the NHL model.

Completion: two or three named tasks.

### 2. Turn each requirement into an ability

For each stated requirement, the ability it stands for, tied to a task. "Stats PhD" becomes "can tell an edge from noise on 200 games". "Five years at a sportsbook" becomes "can read and fix an odds feed". Drop any requirement with no ability behind it, and add any ability a task needs that no requirement covered.

Completion: three to five abilities, each with the requirement it replaced or "new".

### 3. Name the evidence and the level

For each ability: what would show it directly, such as code they wrote, a dated public model record or a talk-through of a real company problem, and what good enough looks like. No puzzles. Any sample uses public or made-up data; no keys.

Completion: one piece of evidence and one plain level per ability.

### 4. Decide the brief

Keep, change or stop the brief, with the rewritten requirements. The owner approves; name who updates the brief. Draft only; post and send nothing.

Completion: the verdict word, the rewritten requirements, the owner and nothing posted.

## Anti-Patterns

- A degree or school as a hard filter.
- Years of experience with no ability named.
- Brainteasers in place of real company work.
- Writing the bar after seeing a favorite applicant.
