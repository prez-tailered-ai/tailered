# Gain share rule

Share the gains with everyone who does the work, by a written rule. Walton started an associate profit-sharing plan in 1971 and let associates buy stock; he credited much of Walmart's growth to people who owned part of it. The guard: associate pay was widely reported as low, and a share paid only in stock can fall. At the company the share sits on top of fair pay, never in place of it, and is paid in cash first.

## When to Activate

- The owner asks how the people doing the work share in gains.
- A contractor, creator or staff member asks what they get if the company grows.
- A bonus, profit share or equity grant is proposed with no written rule.

Do not use for:
- one creator's lowest revenue share to stay: tailered-ai:creator-share-floor;
- what members pay: tailered-ai:price-on-value;
- bringing in an outside builder: tailered-ai:bring-in-the-builders.

## Inputs and outputs

Where to work: create a task folder, `artifacts/<skill name>-<YYYYMMDD>/` in the current project (this session's scratchpad if the project is read-only), and write the output there. Tables go in your reply.

Inputs: who does the work and how each is paid today, last quarter's revenue and costs, and any promise already made.

Missing inputs never leave a blank. Put in a concrete proposal from what you know, mark it `proposal`, finish every step, and end with the one or two inputs that would change the result. A proposal is a real value: a dollar figure, a name, a date, a game, a list of likely items. Writing missing, pending, unknown, TBD or $X in its place is still a blank.

Output: `gain-share-rule.md`: who is in, the gain measure, the rule, and a worked example on last quarter.

## Steps

Lead the reply with the worked example table, then the verdict line, then the rule.

### 1. List who does the work

Name every person or group: the owner, staff, contractors and model creators. Agent sessions are tools, not sharers. Record any promise already made, word for word.

Completion: a list with each person's role, current pay basis and any existing promise.

### 2. Define the gain

Pick one gain number taken from records, not judgment (proposal: 10% of quarterly operating profit above zero goes to the pool). Say where the number comes from and who checks it.

Completion: the gain measure, its source, the pool percentage and the checker are written.

### 3. Write the rule

State who is in, how the pool splits (by hours, by revenue a person's work drove, or equal), when it pays, and what happens in a quarter with no gain. Pay in cash first; stock or options only on top, with the risk stated.

Completion: a rule of ten lines or fewer that a stranger could apply to last quarter and reach the same payouts.

### 4. Test it and hand it to the owner

Run the rule on last quarter, or on proposal figures, and show each person's share. The owner decides adopt, change or stop. A lawyer or accountant reviews it before anything is signed. Nothing is paid, promised or sent here.

Completion: a worked table, the decision left with the owner, and the outside reviewer named.

## Anti-Patterns

- A share in place of fair pay.
- A rule that needs the owner's judgment each quarter to compute.
- Paying only in stock that can fall.
- Promising a share in a message before the rule is written.
