# Two-way door call

Sort the decision first. Bezos' 2015 letter split decisions into one-way doors, hard to undo and made slowly, and two-way doors, easy to undo and made fast. His 2016 letter said most decisions should be made with about 70% of the information you wish you had. The costly mistake is calling a one-way door two-way: anything members saw, data deleted, money spent or a public post cannot be walked back.

## When to Activate

- A main product tweak, card layout or setting is stuck waiting for more data.
- The owner says "just decide", "two-way door", "one-way door" or "seventy percent".
- Before a deletion, a purchase, a message, a public post, a model swap or a price change.

Do not use for:
- carrying the change through release: tailered-ai:workflow;
- writing the case for a one-way door: tailered-ai:memo-not-slides.

## Inputs and outputs

Where to work: create a task folder, `artifacts/<skill name>-<YYYYMMDD>/` in the current project (this session's scratchpad if the project is read-only), and write the output there. Tables go in your reply.

Inputs: the decision and what is known about it.

Missing inputs never leave a blank. Put in a concrete proposal from what you know, mark it `proposal`, finish every step, and end with the one or two inputs that would change the result.

Output: one decision record in the reply: door type with the four answers, the call, and either the rollback or what must be true before the owner decides.

## Steps

### 1. Test reversibility

Answer four questions. Can it be put back exactly as it was, within a day, at small cost? After it is put back, does anything stay changed: money someone placed on it, a record or post others keep, a promise made? Does it delete, spend or send anything? Does it change numbers the model has already published? A no on the first, or a yes on any other, makes it a one-way door. Being seen is not enough: a sort order, layout or main product tweak members see and that can be switched back is a two-way door.

Completion: four written answers and the door type.

### 2. Two-way: decide now

Decide with what is known. Name the one or two unknowns and the signal that would reverse the call. Name the rollback step and who runs it (the session or person that shipped it, through the same release path), and a date to look back.

Completion: the call, the rollback and the look-back date.

### 3. One-way: slow down

Write the case with tailered-ai:memo-not-slides. Look for a reversible version: stage it, copy instead of move, archive instead of delete, or try one league with tailered-ai:one-league-trial. The owner decides. Do not run it here.

Completion: a reversible alternative offered, and the decision left with the owner.

### 4. Name the route

Fast means less study, not fewer gates. A live change, even a two-way door, still ships through tailered-ai:workflow and the release coordinator on the owner's word.

Completion: the release route is named.

## Anti-Patterns

- Calling a change two-way because it is small.
- Waiting for complete data on a two-way door.
- Skipping the release word because the change is reversible.
- Deleting because "we have backups" without checking the backup.
