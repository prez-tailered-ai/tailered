# Mission pitch

Recruit on the problem, not the salary. SpaceX and Tesla hired on getting to Mars and on clean energy, often below big-tech pay, as Ashlee Vance's 2015 biography reports. The guard: in November 2022 Twitter staff were told to pledge to long, intense hours or leave, and many left. The mission brings people in; honest pay and hours keep them.

## When to Activate

- A job post, contractor brief, creator invitation or outreach note is being written.
- The owner asks how to get great people without big salaries, or to sell them on the mission.
- A recruit asks what the work is really like.

Do not use for:
- writing the role's requirements: tailered-ai:ability-over-credential;
- choosing between candidates with a work sample: tailered-ai:work-sample-hire;
- testing one candidate across several kinds of work: tailered-ai:generalist-stretch-test;
- bringing in an outside person or team for their skill: tailered-ai:bring-in-the-builders;
- setting a creator's revenue share: tailered-ai:creator-share-floor;
- deciding who owns an initiative: tailered-ai:single-threaded-owner.

## Inputs and outputs

Where to work: create a task folder, `artifacts/<skill name>-<YYYYMMDD>/` in the current project (this session's scratchpad if the project is read-only), and write the output there. Tables go in your reply.

Inputs: the role, the hard problem it owns, the pay range, the expected hours, and who the note goes to.

Missing inputs never leave a blank. Put in a concrete proposal from what you know, mark it `proposal`, finish every step, and end with the one or two inputs that would change the result.

Output: `pitch.md`: the draft, the pay and hours block, the number check, the test task with its reviewer, and the verdict.

## Steps

### 1. Name the hard problem

Write one sentence on the problem the person will own, in the company's terms: an NHL model that beats the closing line, or one day build that runs every league. Name the number they would move.

Completion: one problem sentence with a number in it.

### 2. Write honest pay and hours

State the pay range, any equity, the expected weekly hours and on-call. Never write "competitive"; an unknown range is a proposal marked `proposal`. Check every number in the pitch: member counts, revenue, funding, record. The owner's rule from "funding secured": never put a number in front of a recruit before it is real. Remove any number that is not.

Completion: pay range, hours and on-call written, and every number in the pitch either real with its source or removed.

### 3. Add a test of fit

Add one small paid task or question tied to the hard problem that shows whether the person wants it. Give it a time limit and name who reviews it.

Completion: one task with a time limit and a named reviewer.

### 4. Draft and hand to the owner

Lead with the problem, then pay and hours, then the test. The company publishes estimates, never advice; the pitch promises no winnings. Give a verdict of send, change or hold. Nothing is posted or sent from here; the owner sends it.

Completion: a draft saved and send, change or hold left with the owner.

## Anti-Patterns

- Using the mission to cover low pay.
- Asking for unlimited hours.
- Quoting members, revenue or funding that are not real yet.
- Selling picks or winnings.
