# Walk away first

Gates went into the IBM deal holding what IBM needed and kept the rights. Leverage starts with a walk-away written before you talk. The guard is the owner's not-to-copy: in 2000 a court ruled Microsoft kept its monopoly partly through restrictive deals with PC makers and internet providers. The company keeps leverage through alternatives, never by locking rivals out.

## When to Activate

- A deal talk is coming.
- The owner says "negotiate", "what do we ask for" or "keep the leverage".
- The other side wants exclusivity or a yes today.

Do not use for:
- reading a term sheet for caps: tailered-ai:growth-cap-check;
- licensing the company's own work out: tailered-ai:license-dont-sell;
- a long-time supplier: tailered-ai:partner-for-decades;
- an investor asking for control: tailered-ai:keep-the-final-say.

## Inputs and outputs

Where to work: create a task folder, `artifacts/<skill name>-<YYYYMMDD>/` in the current project (this session's scratchpad if the project is read-only), and write the output there. Tables go in your reply.

Inputs: the other side, what each wants, and the talk date.

Missing inputs never leave a blank. Put in a concrete proposal from what you know, mark it `proposal`, finish every step, and end with the one or two inputs that would change the result. A proposal is a real value: a dollar figure, a name, a date, a game, a list of likely items. Writing missing, pending, unknown, TBD or $X in its place is still a blank.

Output: `deal-prep.md`: leverage, alternative, walk-away, ask, trades, verdict.

## Steps

Your reply leads with the leverage table (what each side needs from the other, the company's alternative), then the verdict line: talk, change or walk.

### 1. Map the leverage

List what the company has that they need, such as data or audience, and the reverse. Name the company's best alternative with no deal, and its cost in dollars or weeks.

Completion: both lists, and the alternative with a number.

### 2. Write the walk-away first

Before any offer, write where the owner says no: lowest fee, longest term, any loss of ownership or final say. It is a proposal until the owner sets it.

Completion: a walk-away with a number, set by the owner or marked proposal.

### 3. Plan the ask and the trades

Write the opening ask, above the walk-away, and at most three cheap trades, such as a longer term for a lower price. Ownership and dealing with others stay off the table.

Completion: an opening ask with a number and at most three trades.

### 4. Check the rival guard

Strike any ask that makes the other side drop, bar or charge the company's rivals, or ties products together to block a competitor.

Completion: each struck ask listed, or "none found".

### 5. Verdict and owner

Verdict: talk, change or walk, and who leads the talk. Only the owner agrees to terms; nothing is signed or sent here. A live change goes through tailered-ai:workflow and the release coordinator on the owner's word.

Completion: the verdict, the owner and the route named.

## Anti-Patterns

- Talking before the walk-away is written.
- Giving exclusivity for speed.
- Asks that hurt rivals instead of helping members.
- Moving the walk-away mid-talk without the owner.
