# Bring in the builders

Bring in the people who already built what the company needs, for their skill, on a capped trial first. Facebook bought FriendFeed in 2009 and Drop.io in 2010 largely for their teams; FriendFeed's Bret Taylor later became Facebook's CTO. The guards: FriendFeed was shut down on its users in 2015, and Instagram's and WhatsApp's founders left in 2018, reported as over lost autonomy. So promises to the builders and to their users are written down, and access starts small.

## When to Activate

- The owner wants an outside person or small team: contractor, freelancer, advisor, modeler, tool maker.
- The owner says "hire them", "get the people who built it" or "we need someone who knows X".
- Before an outsider gets access to code, data or keys.

Do not use for:
- writing the role's requirements: tailered-ai:ability-over-credential;
- writing the pitch or outreach note: tailered-ai:mission-pitch;
- choosing between candidates with a work sample: tailered-ai:work-sample-hire;
- testing one candidate across several kinds of work: tailered-ai:generalist-stretch-test;
- licensing their tool instead of the people: tailered-ai:license-not-rebuild;
- finding the money to pay them: tailered-ai:find-it-inside;
- naming who owns an initiative inside the company: tailered-ai:single-threaded-owner.

## Inputs and outputs

Where to work: create a task folder, `artifacts/<skill name>-<YYYYMMDD>/` in the current project (this session's scratchpad if the project is read-only), and write the output there. Tables go in your reply.

Inputs: the person or team, the skill the company lacks, their rate if known, and any product they run with users.

Missing inputs never leave a blank. Put in a concrete proposal from what you know, mark it `proposal`, finish every step, and end with the one or two inputs that would change the result.

Output: `builder-trial.md`: the gap, the trial with its cap and check, the access and ownership terms, the written promises, and the verdict.

## Steps

### 1. Name the gap

Say the one skill the company lacks, and how long it would take to grow inside, in hours or months. If it is under a month of work inside, say so; the trial may not be worth it.

Completion: the skill in one sentence and the inside estimate as a number.

### 2. Set a capped trial

One paid project of two to six weeks, with a cap in dollars and a result a command or file decides, such as the matcher passes on last season's games. Mark guessed numbers `proposal`.

Completion: the project, the cap in dollars, the end date and the check.

### 3. Limit access and write the promises

During the trial: a copied folder, no production keys, no member data. The company owns the work. Write what the owner promises them (say over their area, credit) and what happens to their users if they run a product, with notice and an export before anything shuts. Name the company owner who works with them.

Completion: the access list, the ownership line, the written promises and the named owner.

### 4. Judge at the end date

Give one verdict: bring in, extend the trial once, or part ways, with the check result. The owner decides, signs and pays; this skill hires, pays, invites and grants access to no one. Their work ships through tailered-ai:workflow and the release coordinator on the owner's word.

Completion: the verdict, the check result, and the release route named.

## Anti-Patterns

- Hiring for a resume instead of a gap.
- Production keys or member data on day one.
- Promises to the builders made out loud only.
- Shutting their product on its users with no notice.
